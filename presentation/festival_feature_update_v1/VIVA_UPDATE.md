# Viva update: explicit festival features

Use these answers with the new explicit-feature supplement. The earlier multi-festival notes describe the preceding diagnostic checkpoint and should not be used to say the added-feature experiment is still unperformed.

**What new experiment did you complete?** We supplied five family-specific festival-day or fixed-window indicators to covariate-capable models. We compared them with ordinary-calendar, same-size zero and active placebo inputs on identical cases. Relative-day channels were an additional classical-model sensitivity.

**Did the features help?** In the historical next-day anchor summary, day flags reduced Ridge MASE from 1.244 to 0.561 and SARIMAX from 1.310 to 0.532. TimesFM improved from 1.348 to 1.103, while Chronos changed more modestly from 1.371 to 1.329. Some individual festivals and longer-lead results worsened. Broad windows were less reliable. These are error scores, not accuracy percentages.

**Is SARIMAX now the best model overall?** It has the lowest next-day day-flag error among the main configurations shown in this particular anchor experiment. That is not a universal ranking. The older broad daily benchmark and full-window comparisons use different cases and answer different questions.

**How much data supports this comparison?** There are 24 festival/state/year anchor targets across 2024–2025, each scored at three lead times, giving 72 cases per arm. Nearby days, states, years and repeated targets across leads are dependent. The classical secondary study covers wider windows; the foundation added-feature study is anchor-focused.

**What did the controls show?** Zero-valued channels can change foundation-model forecasts without event information. Active placebo results and all failures are retained. The large aggregate Ridge/SARIMAX day-flag gains exceed their zero/placebo changes, but these bounded controls do not establish causality or a new universal mechanism.

**Why did default trees show weak gains?** Some annual day indicators have only one or two positive training examples, below the selected minimum leaf size. We separately tested a fixed leaf minimum of two with its own calendar baseline and reported every arm. We did not select that regime using the evaluated errors.

**What calendar issue did you find?** Karnataka's annual 2023 notice lists Ganesh on 18 September, while Maharashtra lists 19 September; Maharashtra's Holi holiday is 7 March. The new named training/context ledger follows annual notices rather than silently substituting library dates. Full amendment certification remains open.

**Did adding flags harm other days?** A separate weekday/season-matched non-event sample showed no aggregate next-day day-flag penalty, but some lead-14 errors rose, including Chronos by about 0.026 MASE. This is not an exhaustive all-day evaluation.

**Did you prove that separate festival identities are necessary?** No. We tested these typed-date features against ordinary calendar, zeros and placebos. A pooled-event comparator was not run, so typed identities cannot be declared superior to a generic event flag.

**How did you check leakage and correctness?** We fixed dates/settings before the new intervention outputs, kept fitting labels before cutoffs, retained original pre-year parameter budgets, and matched cases and scales. All 1,296 foundation task inputs reconstruct byte-for-byte in a separate implementation. Thirty-seven tests and metric/fitting audits pass. Historical publication timing and pretraining/proxy overlap remain unresolved.

**Are the uncertainty forecasts calibrated?** That has not been established. Eight native Chronos adjacent-quantile crossing pairs are retained across the new arms/cohorts; TimesFM has none in these scored outputs. A passed point-error audit is not a calibration certificate.

**Is the project journal-ready or prospectively validated?** No. Rights response, amendment review, independent human review and sufficient independent confirmation remain pending. The original future forecasts are unchanged; complete 31 October–11 November outcomes cannot be scored for a 1 November presentation. Present the study as ongoing research.
