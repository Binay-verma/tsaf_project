# Journal research assessment and next study

## Decision

The project is worth continuing as an empirical forecasting and calendar-quality study. It is **not yet a journal-ready new-method paper**. Working code, thousands of forecasts and additional runtime are insufficient by themselves. The current festival encoding worsens Chronos-2's average short-horizon festival error; that negative result must remain visible. Neither zero mistakes nor journal acceptance can be promised.

The useful research question is: **How much do regional calendar provenance and event representation affect transfer of pretrained forecasting models to Indian state electricity series, under strict temporal evaluation?** This is a candidate contribution, not an established novelty claim. The study must separate calendar information, representation, feature count, model adaptation and regional transfer. It must also distinguish daily supplied energy from unconstrained demand or hourly peak load.

## Search and evidence scope

Primary-source searches covered calendar encodings, learned event covariates, covariate uncertainty, Indian moving holidays, foundation-model evaluation leakage and Indian load implementations. Three especially close 2026 papers were downloaded and their methods read: calendar encodings, impact-driven event covariates and load forecasting under covariate uncertainty. Other entries below were inspected through primary abstracts, official documentation or the original repository. This is a focused novelty audit, **not an exhaustive systematic review**. Dates, versions and venue status must be rechecked before manuscript submission. A preprint is not presented as a peer-reviewed journal article. No secondary-summary numerical claim is used as our experimental evidence.

## What the completed experiments actually tell us

Read `VALIDATION_REPORT.md` for generated tables. Chronos-2 history-only gives the lowest aggregate 2026 MASE among the tested configurations at horizons 1, 7 and 14. This is conditional on the chosen training/refit schedule and limited feature set. The LightGBM addition uses an eight-configuration pre-2026 validation search, not an exhaustive or state-of-the-art optimisation. Its poor performance remains in the report. It uses the same six history features as Ridge; richer seasonal, trend, weather and rolling-history predictors remain untested. Its minimum leaf-size constraint can suppress rare binary festival splits, so identical B/D outputs are not evidence that festival information is intrinsically useless.

The current positive festival finding belongs to short-horizon Ridge/SARIMAX comparisons. Chronos-2's seven additional binary channels worsen mean day-1 festival MAE by 4.62% relative to ordinary calendar inputs. Four annual festival clusters are too few for broad generalisation. A regional calendar correction, feature-count change and encoding change currently occur together; attributing the result to any one mechanism would be unjustified.

The source contains **energy met/supplied**, so it can reflect supply constraints. It is not a direct measurement of latent unconstrained demand, household use or hourly peak load. No downstream dispatch or economic benefit has been measured.

## Study design that could support a stronger paper

1. **Calendar information and provenance.** Build an event ledger with state, district scope, religious/office/bank definition, initial notification, amendment, issue date and effective date. Separate annual-as-issued from final amended observance. Have dates checked independently by a human familiar with the regions. A general office closure is a covariate, not proof of statewide electricity response. Complete all event dates actually used in both training and testing. Resolve source redistribution rights before public data release.
2. **Controlled representation experiment.** Cross the same event information with binary flag, fixed-width relative-day channels, scalar relative-day/cyclic encoding and strictly past-only target encoding. Include equal-dimensional zero-channel controls and weekday/season-matched placebo events, with seeds and construction frozen before outcome inspection. Shift dates +/-1 and +/-2 days as sensitivity checks. If placebo channels show similar changes, do not credit event information. Target encoding must be recalculated using only observations preceding each origin, with shrinkage and unseen-event fallback chosen on development years.
3. **Broader but justified coverage.** Audit the existing full state snapshot before selecting more states; do not assume all 144 columns are comparable state targets. Include multiple major events where records support them, rather than selecting events with large errors after looking at targets. Obtain an independently licensed hourly dataset for an additional resolution/domain test, with unit, time zone, timestamp convention, aggregation and missingness audits. Do not duplicate source-derived national series and call them independent datasets.
4. **Fair models and adaptation budgets.** Compare at least two independently developed, covariate-capable TSFMs, each with pinned actual weights and separate overlap audit. Add ETS/dynamic regression, a stronger train-only-tuned gradient-boosting baseline with richer histories, and a small trained neural baseline if resources permit. Compare annual versus monthly/weekly local refits; the current annual fitting versus rolling TSFM context is a deployment-policy comparison, not an architecture-only experiment. Give all learned encodings and local models an explicit equal development budget. Include run time and memory.
5. **Transfer and honest temporal tests.** Historical 2022–2025 and the now-inspected 2026 slice are exploratory development evidence. Use leave-one-region-out and leave-one-event-year-out evaluation; never tune on the region/year being scored. Reserve a new source or prospectively arriving dates for final confirmation. A model trained after an event cannot use that event for a clean post-weight test. The 2026 Diwali outcome does not yet exist in our snapshot. Freeze prospective predictions when observations through the origin become available; do not retrospectively backfill them and label them prospective.
6. **Uncertainty and robustness.** Primary point score: event-balanced MAE scaled per state using past weekly differences. Also report all-day MASE, event bias, worst-event errors and non-event regression. Keep the same cases for all ablations. Save quantile forecasts, score pinball/scaled quantile loss and interval coverage/width, and calibrate using past data only. Resample event years or event blocks with state dependence retained, not thousands of nearby days as independent trials. With few clusters, emphasise effect sizes, per-year plots and uncertainty rather than fragile significance claims. Prespecify a primary contrast and control secondary multiplicity.
7. **Mechanism, failure and reproducibility.** Plot event-relative observed and forecast profiles by region/year, test calendar-date perturbations, inspect model covariate sensitivity, and show cases where features hurt. Preserve each run, input hash, prediction file, selection log and failed fit. Use an independently executed clean-environment rerun and human review before submission.

No causal statement about festivals is licensed by predictive ablations: weather, supply, economic activity and other co-occurring changes remain confounders. Perfect future observed weather must never be passed as operationally available weather.

## A realistic sequence and decision gates

| Phase | Work | Exit evidence | Approximate active effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Final novelty matrix, data rights, target definition, calendar amendment ledger | Auditable sources and a specific contribution distinct from the closest papers | 3–5 days |
| 2 | Equal-information encodings, placebo controls, richer local baselines, second TSFM | Leakage-tested implementation; fixed development budgets and run registry | 5–8 days |
| 3 | Regional/event transfer, resolution sensitivity, uncertainty and failure analysis | Consistent case coverage, event-block uncertainty, negative findings retained | 5–8 days |
| 4 | Untouched/prospective confirmation, independent rerun, manuscript and revision | Reproduced evidence, no unresolved data rights or unsupported novelty claims | 5–10 days plus time for new observations |

These are planning estimates of roughly 3–6 active weeks, not work already performed and not an acceptance timeline. More clock time does not make a weak contribution strong. New festival evidence can require waiting for a calendar event or obtaining another historical source with appropriate model cutoffs.

Proceed as a methods paper only if the proposed mechanism survives equal-information controls and unseen-region/event tests. If calendar provenance is the main contribution, pursue a documented benchmark/data-quality paper after rights and coverage are resolved. If neither survives, report a useful replication/negative-result study instead of inventing improvements. A negative result can be valuable when it changes evaluation practice and is robustly demonstrated.

## Manuscript and journal strategy

Working title: **Regional Calendar Provenance and Event Representation in Foundation-Model Forecasting of Indian Supplied Electricity**. The title is provisional and does not assert novelty or superiority.

A defensible manuscript would contain: explicit research questions and nearest-work differences; source/target and date ledger; availability/weight-overlap protocol; model and encoding controls; all-day and event results with transfer tests; probabilistic and robustness analysis; compute and reproducibility; limitations and public release terms. The current results belong in a preliminary-results section, not a final abstract announcing a successful new method.

The *International Journal of Forecasting* publisher description explicitly includes energy forecasting, empirical evaluation and reproducibility. *Journal of Forecasting* welcomes methodological and practical applications. Those are scope matches to investigate after the contribution is demonstrated, not acceptance predictions. An energy-focused venue would additionally need convincing operational relevance, ideally hourly behaviour or a decision-based evaluation. Do not choose a venue merely because it promises fast acceptance. Check the live author requirements and publication charges only after choosing a justified target.

Submission gates remain open: distinct contribution; adequate independent event evidence; complete dates used; explicit data reuse rights; fair baselines; model-specific overlap audit; fresh confirmation; calibrated uncertainty; independent rerun and author review. **The project is not submitted or publication-ready at this checkpoint.**
