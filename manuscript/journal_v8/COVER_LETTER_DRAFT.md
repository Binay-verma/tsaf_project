I submit the manuscript "Typed or pooled? Festival covariates in foundation and local forecasting models: unstable orderings, matched controls and a combination hedge" for consideration as a research article.

Forecasters who add holiday or festival indicators must choose whether each event gets its own channel or shares one with other events. Time-series foundation models make this choice more consequential, because the forecaster controls the covariate channels but does not re-estimate the model. The manuscript compares typed and pooled festival indicators for two pinned foundation models and four local models on daily electricity in three Indian states, with matched calendar, zero-channel and weekday-placebo controls, a source-driven correction of a festival date, and a separately registered replication on New York City transit ridership.

The main findings are:

- the better representation reverses between event years, models and data sources, and no typed-versus-pooled difference survives cluster-level sign-flip tests with Holm adjustment;
- daily adaptations of Ziel's holiday-as-Sunday and replacing designs improve on a calendar baseline in India, as additive flags do, but not in NYC event windows;
- following the previous year's better representation performed no better than chance, while an equal-weight average of the typed and pooled forecasts had lower regret.

The study is retrospective and limited to two Indian event years and two transit observances; the manuscript states these limits and does not claim a general ranking of models or representations. Code, protocols, forecasts, failed attempts and audits are public at https://github.com/Binay-verma/tsaf_project . The reuse terms of the Indian source compilation are still being confirmed with its curator, as described in the data availability statement.
